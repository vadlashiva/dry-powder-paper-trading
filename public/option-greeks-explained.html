Option Greeks explained simply: Delta, Theta, Gamma and Vega

The Greeks are just numbers that describe how an option's price reacts to different things. Learn the four that matter and the options chain stops looking like a wall of noise.

Updated 31 October 2026

Delta: how much the premium moves with NIFTY

Delta tells you roughly how many rupees the premium moves for a ₹1 move in the underlying. An ATM call usually has a delta near 0.50; deep ITM calls approach 1; far OTM calls approach 0. Puts have negative deltas.

Delta itself changes as NIFTY moves — that's Gamma.

Theta: the daily cost of holding

Theta is how much value the option loses per day purely from time passing. A theta of −8 means the premium shrinks by about ₹8 per unit per day — about ₹520 per NIFTY lot — if nothing else changes. Theta gets larger as expiry approaches, which is why option buyers fight the clock and option sellers like it.

Gamma: how fast delta changes

Gamma measures how quickly delta changes as the index moves. It's highest for ATM options close to expiry. High gamma means an option can go from nearly worthless to valuable (or the reverse) very quickly — exciting for buyers, dangerous for sellers.

Vega and implied volatility

Implied volatility (IV) is the market's estimate of how much the index will swing. Higher IV means pricier options. Vega tells you how much the premium changes for a 1-point change in IV. Before big events (budgets, elections, RBI meetings) IV often rises; afterwards it often falls sharply, and premiums can drop even when the index moves in your favour.

Putting it together

Greeks are estimates from a pricing model (usually Black-Scholes) and change constantly. Use them to understand risk, not as exact predictions.

Common questions

Which option Greek is most important for beginners?

Delta and theta. Delta shows how much your option moves with the index; theta shows how much it loses each day you hold it.

Why did my option lose value when NIFTY went up?

Usually because of time decay (theta) or a fall in implied volatility (vega) outweighing a small favourable move, or because the option was far out of the money.

What is a good delta for buying options?

Many beginners choose options with a delta around 0.4–0.6 (near the money), which react meaningfully to the index without the very low odds of far OTM options.
