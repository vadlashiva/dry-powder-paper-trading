How to use RSI and MACD without overtrading

RSI and MACD are the two most popular indicators on Indian trading screens. Both describe momentum — how strongly price has been moving — and both are easy to misuse.

Updated 31 October 2026

RSI: Relative Strength Index

RSI compares recent gains with recent losses and scales the result from 0 to 100. The standard setting looks at the last 14 candles.

  • Above 70: often called overbought — price has risen quickly.
  • Below 30: often called oversold — price has fallen quickly.
  • Around 50: momentum is balanced.

The trap: "overbought" doesn't mean "about to fall". In a strong uptrend RSI can stay above 70 for weeks. Many traders instead use RSI to confirm a trend — buying pullbacks when RSI dips toward 40–50 in an uptrend rather than shorting every reading above 70.

Divergence

If price makes a new high but RSI makes a lower high, upward momentum is weakening. Divergence isn't a sell signal by itself, but it's a reason to tighten your stop or avoid adding.

MACD: Moving Average Convergence Divergence

MACD tracks the gap between two moving averages of price — by default the 12-period and 26-period exponential averages. It has three parts:

  • MACD line: the 12 EMA minus the 26 EMA.
  • Signal line: a 9-period average of the MACD line.
  • Histogram: the distance between the two, shown as bars.

A bullish crossover happens when the MACD line crosses above the signal line; a bearish one when it crosses below. Crossovers above the zero line (in an uptrend) tend to be more meaningful than those deep below it.

Using them together

  1. Decide the trend first, for example with price above a rising 50-day average.
  2. Wait for a pullback where RSI cools toward 40–50.
  3. Look for MACD's histogram to turn back up, or a bullish crossover.
  4. Enter with a stop-loss below the pullback low.

Common mistakes

  • Indicator overload. RSI, MACD and stochastics all measure momentum. Adding more doesn't add information.
  • Ignoring price. Indicators are calculated from price and always lag it.
  • Trading every signal on a 1-minute chart. Short timeframes generate constant, mostly meaningless crossovers.
Indicators describe what price has done; they don't predict what it will do. Test any rule over many past days before trusting it.

Common questions

What are the best RSI settings?

The standard 14-period RSI with 70/30 levels is a reasonable default. Changing settings rarely helps as much as using RSI in the context of the trend.

Is MACD good for intraday trading?

It can be used intraday, but on very short timeframes it gives many false crossovers. Many traders check a higher timeframe for direction first.

Should I sell when RSI is above 70?

Not automatically. In strong uptrends RSI can stay above 70 for a long time. Overbought readings are a caution sign, not a sell signal.
