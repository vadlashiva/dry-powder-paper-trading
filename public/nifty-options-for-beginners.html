NIFTY options for beginners: calls, puts, lot size and expiry

NIFTY options are the most traded contracts in India, and the most misunderstood. This guide covers what you're really buying, what it costs, and how you can lose money even when you guess the direction right.

Updated 31 October 2026

Calls and puts in one minute

An option is a contract that gains or loses value as NIFTY moves.

  • A call option (CE) gains value when NIFTY rises above its strike price.
  • A put option (PE) gains value when NIFTY falls below its strike price.

The price you pay is the premium, quoted per unit. Options trade in fixed lots: since January 2026 one NIFTY lot is 65 units, so your cost is premium × 65.

Buy 1 lot NIFTY 24000 CENIFTY at 23,950

Buying an option can never lose more than the premium paid. Selling one can — see option buying vs selling.

Strike, moneyness and expiry

Strike price is the NIFTY level the option is built around. A call with a strike below the current index is in the money (ITM); one above it is out of the money (OTM); the strike nearest the index is at the money (ATM). OTM options are cheaper because NIFTY has to move further before they're worth anything at expiry.

Expiry is the date the contract ends. NIFTY has weekly and monthly expiries; the exact weekday is set by NSE and has changed in the past, so check the current calendar. Most of an option's time value disappears in the last few days before expiry.

Why you can be right and still lose

  • Time decay (theta): every day that passes, the option loses some value, even if NIFTY doesn't move. A slow move in your direction may not be enough.
  • Volatility (vega): when expected volatility falls — often right after a big event — premiums shrink across the board.
  • Distance: a cheap, far OTM option needs a large move just to break even. Most expire worthless.
SEBI's study of individual F&O traders found about 9 in 10 lost money between FY22 and FY24. Practise until lot sizes, time decay and breakevens feel obvious before using real money.

A sensible way to start practising

  1. Trade one lot at a time, ATM or slightly ITM, with at least a week to expiry.
  2. Decide your exit premium before you buy (for example, 30% below what you paid).
  3. Watch theta: note the premium each morning even when NIFTY is flat.

Common questions

What is the lot size of NIFTY options?

Since January 2026, one NIFTY options lot is 65 units. Your cost is the premium multiplied by 65. Lot sizes are revised by NSE from time to time, so check the current figure.

How much money do I need to buy a NIFTY option?

Only the premium for one lot: for example ₹120 × 65 = ₹7,800. Selling (writing) options needs much more, because the exchange blocks margin.

Can I lose more than I invested when buying options?

No. When you buy a call or put, the most you can lose is the premium you paid. Option sellers face much larger potential losses.
