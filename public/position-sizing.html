Position sizing: how many shares should you buy?

Choosing what to buy gets all the attention, but how much you buy decides whether a bad trade is a scratch or a disaster. Here's the one formula you need.

Updated 31 October 2026

The formula

Decide how much of your capital you're willing to lose on one trade — commonly 1% — and how far away your stop-loss is. Then:

Shares to buy = (capital × risk %) ÷ (entry price − stop-loss price)

This flips the usual approach. Instead of "I'll buy ₹1 lakh worth and see", you start from the loss you can accept and work backwards.

A wider stop means fewer shares. With the stop at ₹450, the same ₹10,000 risk allows only 200 shares.

Add a cap on position size

The formula can suggest very large positions when the stop is close. Add a second rule so no single stock dominates your account — for example, no position larger than 20–25% of capital. Take whichever gives the smaller number of shares.

Why 1–2% risk per trade?

Losing streaks happen to everyone. Risking 1% per trade, ten losses in a row costs you about 10% of your account — painful but recoverable. Risking 10% per trade, the same streak wipes out about two-thirds of it, and you'd need to triple what's left just to get back to where you started.

Risk per tradeAfter 10 losses in a rowGain needed to recover
1%≈ 90% of capital left≈ 11%
2%≈ 82% left≈ 22%
5%≈ 60% left≈ 67%
10%≈ 35% left≈ 187%

Options are different

With options you buy whole lots, not single shares, so sizing happens in lots. One NIFTY lot is 65 units: an option priced at ₹120 costs ₹7,800 per lot. If you'd exit at ₹84 (a 30% fall in premium), the risk is ₹36 × 65 = ₹2,340 per lot. Divide your rupee risk by that number to get the lots to trade, and round down.

Common questions

What percentage of capital should I risk per trade?

Many traders risk 0.5–2% of their capital per trade. Beginners are usually better off at the low end until they have a tested method.

Is position sizing the same as diversification?

No. Diversification spreads money across different holdings. Position sizing decides how much to put into each trade based on how much you could lose.

How do I size an options trade?

Work out the rupee loss per lot at your exit price (premium drop × lot size), then divide your maximum risk by that amount and round down to whole lots.
