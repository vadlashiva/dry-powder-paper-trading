Market, limit and stop orders explained

Every trade starts with an order. Knowing which type to use, and what it does when the market moves fast, saves money from your very first trade.

Updated 31 October 2026

Market order: "buy it now"

A market order buys or sells immediately at the best price available. It always fills on a liquid stock, but you don't control the exact price. For large stocks like HDFC Bank or TCS the difference is usually a few paise; for thinly traded stocks it can be much more.

Use it when getting in or out matters more than the last few paise.

Limit order: "only at this price or better"

A limit buy order fills only at your price or lower; a limit sell only at your price or higher. If the market never reaches it, nothing happens.

Limit buy on TCSExample
Current price₹4,105
Your limit buy price₹4,080
If TCS dips to ₹4,080 or lowerOrder fills at ₹4,080 or better
If TCS never dips that farNo trade — order stays pending

Use it when you have a specific price in mind and are happy to miss the trade if it isn't reached.

Stop orders: "act once the price crosses this level"

A stop order sits quietly until the price reaches its trigger, then becomes an order to buy or sell. Its most common use is the stop-loss: "if my stock falls to ₹1,372, sell it."

  • SL-M (stop-loss market): once triggered, it becomes a market order. It will fill, but in a fast fall the price may be lower than your trigger.
  • SL (stop-loss limit): once triggered, it becomes a limit order at a price you set. You control the worst price, but in a sharp fall it may not fill at all — leaving you holding a losing position.

Beginners generally prefer the certainty of getting out, which is why PaperRally's stop orders behave like SL-M.

Common questions

What is the difference between a limit order and a stop order?

A limit order fills at your price or better and is used to buy cheaper or sell higher. A stop order waits until the price crosses a trigger and is mostly used to limit losses or enter breakouts.

Why didn't my limit order fill?

The market never traded at your limit price, or there weren't enough sellers (or buyers) at that price before it moved away.

Is a market order dangerous?

On large, liquid stocks the slippage is usually tiny. On illiquid stocks or in very fast markets it can fill noticeably away from the last price, so a limit order is safer there.
