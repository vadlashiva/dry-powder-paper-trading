Option buying vs option selling: risk, margin and probability

Option buyers pay a small premium for a chance at a big gain. Option sellers collect that premium in exchange for taking the big risk. Neither side is free money — here's how each really works.

Updated 31 October 2026

The two sides of every option

For every option bought, someone sold it. The buyer pays the premium and has the right to profit if the market moves enough. The seller (or writer) receives the premium and takes on the obligation, which is where the large risk lies.

A 2.5% rally turned the seller's ₹3,900 income into a ₹22,100 loss. That asymmetry is the whole story of option selling.

Why selling looks easy at first

Most short-dated OTM options expire worthless, so sellers win often. The danger is that one sharp move can wipe out months of small gains. Professional sellers manage this with strict stop-losses, hedges (buying a further OTM option to cap the loss) and small position sizes.

Which should a beginner practise?

  • Start by buying one lot at a time: the loss is capped and you learn how premiums, theta and lot sizes behave.
  • Practise selling only after that, always with a stop-loss, and watch how much margin each trade blocks.
  • Track results over at least 50 trades. A seller's record can look perfect for weeks and then change in a single day.

Common questions

Is option selling more profitable than buying?

Sellers win more often, but their losses can be much larger. Over time, results depend on risk control, not on which side you choose.

How much margin do I need to sell a NIFTY option?

It varies with the strike, volatility and exchange rules, but commonly well over ₹1 lakh per lot without a hedge. Your broker shows the exact margin before you place the order.
